# Ad Teardown

## Purpose

Analyze an existing advertisement or campaign
to understand its strategic and creative
mechanisms.

Do not confuse creative quality with
proven commercial performance.

## Required Inputs

- Brand
- Advertisement or campaign
- Market or geography, if known
- Source material or campaign description
- Business objective, if known
- Available performance evidence, if any

If information is missing, mark it
"Not provided" or "Unknown."

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Source Ledger

Extract the supplied information.

Classify claims as:
- Observed in source
- Verified external fact
- Inference
- Hypothesis
- Unknown

Never invent missing scenes, dialogue,
metrics, partnerships, or campaign results.

### Step 2 — Business Context

Identify:
- Product or service
- Category
- Intended audience
- Likely business objective
- Competitive context
- Consumer problem

Distinguish confirmed objectives from
inferred objectives.

### Step 3 — Creative Breakdown

Analyze:
- Opening hook
- Narrative structure
- Central creative idea
- Emotional trigger
- Visual language
- Sound and music
- Brand integration
- Call to action

Only analyze elements supported by the
provided source material.

### Step 4 — Strategic Mechanism

Explain:
- What consumer tension is being addressed?
- Why might the concept attract attention?
- How is the brand made relevant?
- What makes the idea distinctive?
- What must the audience understand or believe?

Mark psychological interpretations
as hypotheses rather than facts.

### Step 5 — Commercial Evaluation

Assess:
- Product visibility
- Brand attribution
- Message clarity
- Audience relevance
- Conversion path
- Media suitability
- Potential execution constraints

Do not claim the campaign increased sales
without supporting evidence.

### Step 6 — Scorecard

Score each criterion from 1 to 5:

1. Hook strength
2. Message clarity
3. Brand integration
4. Creative distinctiveness
5. Commercial relevance

Provide a justification for each score.

Scores are subjective evaluations,
not measured campaign outcomes.

### Step 7 — Recommendations

Propose:
- Three specific creative improvements
- One alternative creative direction
- One measurable experiment

Explain the trade-offs of each recommendation.

### Step 8 — Final Output

Produce:

1. Source and evidence ledger
2. Campaign summary
3. Business and audience analysis
4. Creative breakdown
5. Strategic mechanism
6. Commercial assessment
7. Five-criteria scorecard
8. Three improvements
9. Alternative creative direction
10. Experiment plan
11. Unknowns and research requirements

## Quality Checks

Before delivery verify:

- [ ] Correct advertisement analyzed
- [ ] No invented scenes or dialogue
- [ ] Facts separated from inferences
- [ ] No fabricated performance metrics
- [ ] Scorecard includes justifications
- [ ] Recommendations are actionable
- [ ] Experiment has an appropriate KPI
- [ ] Missing evidence is acknowledged

## Research Integrity

If the source is a description rather than
the actual advertisement, explicitly state
that the analysis is description-based.

If the source is a video or image, do not
claim to have inspected it unless its
contents were actually accessible.

Never present speculative campaign analysis
as official brand strategy.

## Mandatory Evidence and Scoring Gates

### Source Access

Before analysis, classify source access as:

1. Full advertisement inspected
2. Partial advertisement inspected
3. Verified transcript or detailed description
4. Brief summary only
5. No usable source

Explicitly report the access level.

Never describe a source as directly observed
when it was only mentioned in an assignment.

### Evidence-Based Scoring

Only score a criterion when sufficient
evidence is available.

If the opening cannot be inspected,
mark Hook Strength as N/A.

If product placement cannot be verified,
mark Brand Integration as N/A.

Do not calculate a full /25 score when
any criterion is N/A.

### Recommendation Integrity

Before recommending an improvement:

- Identify the existing creative element.
- Explain the evidence supporting the critique.
- Propose the modification.
- State the trade-off.

If the existing element cannot be verified,
label the recommendation as conditional.

### Experiment Design

Primary KPIs must reflect the stated or
inferred business objective.

Do not invent improvement thresholds.

Define sample-size requirements using
baseline rates, minimum detectable effect,
statistical power, and significance level.

If inputs are missing, state that the
sample size cannot yet be determined.

### Final Quality Audit

- [ ] Source access level declared
- [ ] No unsupported visual observations
- [ ] No unsupported numerical scores
- [ ] No fabricated research or results
- [ ] Recommendations reflect known evidence
- [ ] Experiment targets the business objective
- [ ] Unknown information is explicitly identified

## Source Provenance and Analytical Consistency

### Source Provenance

For each important audiovisual claim, record:

- Exact source
- Whether the source was directly inspected
- Timestamp or source passage, where available
- Confidence level

Reading a description of a video is not
equivalent to watching the video.

Do not claim direct observation unless
the relevant audiovisual content was accessed.

### Technical Claim Verification

Never invent technical measurements,
performance thresholds, or engineering results.

Any proposed technical demonstration must use
verified data or clearly identify the
measurement as requiring validation.

### Scoring Consistency

If all five creative criteria have valid scores,
calculate their arithmetic total out of 25.

If one or more criteria are N/A, do not
report a full /25 score.

Distinguish subjective creative scores
from measured commercial effectiveness.

### Causal Measurement

Do not present attribution tracking as
proof of incremental impact.

Where feasible, use randomized control and
treatment groups to evaluate causal effects.

Specify:
- Unit of randomization
- Primary outcome
- Measurement window
- Baseline and sample-size requirements
- Relevant commercial constraints

### Self-Audit Integrity

Only mark a requirement complete if the
output actually demonstrates compliance.

A completed checklist is not evidence
of correctness by itself.
