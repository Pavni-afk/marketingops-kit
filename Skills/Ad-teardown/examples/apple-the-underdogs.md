# Apple at Work — The Underdogs (2019)

**International Advertising Teardown | United States**

**Brand:** Apple  
**Campaign:** The Underdogs  
**Year:** 2019  
**Category:** Technology / B2B / Branded Entertainment  
**Format:** Approximately three-minute short film  
**Analysis:** Independent creative and commercial strategy evaluation

## 1. Executive Thesis

**The Underdogs transforms a product demonstration into a workplace comedy.**

Instead of listing the capabilities of Macs, iPhones, iPads, and Apple Watch, the advertisement follows four employees trying to deliver an ambitious project under an impossible deadline.

The creative mechanism is simple:

**Workplace pressure creates the need. The Apple ecosystem provides the means. The characters deliver the emotional payoff.**

The film is an example of product integration through narrative causality: the technology matters because it helps move the story forward.

## 3. The Business Problem

### The Category Challenge

Business technology marketing often needs to communicate complicated product capabilities to people with limited attention.

Apple's challenge was to make the value of its connected product ecosystem understandable and desirable in a workplace context.

### Inferred Strategic Objective

Increase the relevance of Apple devices as tools for business collaboration and productivity.

### The Strategic Opportunity

Show a complete workflow rather than isolated product features.

The audience can see different devices working together to solve one recognizable problem.

## 4. Audience and Consumer Insight

### Likely Audience

- Business professionals and collaborative teams.
- Employees who influence workplace technology preferences.
- Business owners and organizational technology decision-makers.

These audience segments are inferred from the campaign's business positioning, not confirmed media-targeting data.

### Working Insight

People do not necessarily aspire to use more productivity tools.

They aspire to accomplish meaningful work with fewer obstacles.

### Strategic Tension

**Employees want to do extraordinary work, but ordinary workplace friction gets in the way.**

Apple dramatizes that tension through a small team facing a disproportionately difficult assignment.

## 5. Narrative Breakdown

### Act I — The Opportunity

Four employees get the chance to pitch a circular pizza box concept.

The opportunity is exciting, but the deadline is daunting.

**Creative function:** Establish characters, stakes, and urgency.

### Act II — The Pressure

The team has approximately 48 hours to turn a rough idea into a professional proposal.

Workplace interruptions, practical obstacles, and interpersonal moments add comedy.

**Creative function:** Make the challenge entertaining and relatable.

### Act III — The Work

The characters use Apple products and software capabilities to collaborate, develop designs, communicate, and prepare their presentation.

**Creative function:** Demonstrate the ecosystem in action.

### Act IV — The Payoff

The team approaches its high-stakes presentation.

The campaign resolves with the brand's workplace proposition.

**Creative function:** Connect the characters' effort and the product ecosystem to the broader Apple at Work message.

## 6. Creative Mechanism

### A. Entertainment Before Explanation

The film invites viewers into a recognizable office story.

Product benefits emerge through events rather than through a conventional sales presentation.

**Why this might work:** Viewers may be more receptive to demonstrations when those demonstrations help resolve a story they are invested in.

This is a hypothesis about audience response, not a measured finding.

### B. The Product as an Enabler

Apple devices do not replace the employees' creativity.

The employees remain responsible for the idea and its execution.

The technology enables their progress.

**Strategic implication:** The campaign sells the capability of the people using the tools, not merely the tools themselves.

### C. Ecosystem as a Competitive Advantage

The story creates opportunities to show multiple Apple devices working within one workflow.

Instead of treating each device as a separate purchase, the film presents the ecosystem as an interconnected working environment.

### D. Comedy as a B2B Creative Device

The characters are imperfect, hurried, and recognizably human.

Humour gives the film a tone that differs from conventional corporate technology demonstrations.

**Strategic implication:** B2B audiences can be addressed as people who enjoy stories, not only as buyers evaluating specifications.

## 7. Brand Integration

| Element | Narrative role | Commercial implication |
|---|---|---|
| Mac | Work and presentation preparation | Workplace productivity |
| iPad and Apple Pencil | Design and visual development | Creative workflows |
| iPhone | Communication and connected tasks | Mobility and collaboration |
| Apple Watch | Part of the connected device environment | Ecosystem breadth |
| Connected features | Help coordinate the team's work | Cross-device utility |

The strength of the integration lies in showing multiple products within one continuous challenge.

**Potential weakness:** Viewers may remember the comedy more clearly than the specific capabilities demonstrated.

That possibility requires testing rather than assumption.

## 9. What the Campaign Does Especially Well

### It Makes the Product Necessary to the Plot

The technology is not merely visible in the background.

The characters use it to make progress.

### It Shows Rather Than Claims

The audience can observe workflows instead of relying entirely on advertising promises.

### It Creates Emotional Identification

The employees are not portrayed as flawless executives.

They are a small team trying to prove themselves.

### It Makes an Ecosystem Tangible

The film communicates cross-device collaboration without requiring a long technical explanation.

## 10. Where the Campaign Could Be Stronger

### Weakness 1 — Length and Attention

A roughly three-minute film requires a greater viewing commitment than short-form digital advertising.

**Trade-off:** More storytelling depth, but greater potential for audience drop-off.

### Weakness 2 — Buyer-Specific Information

The film demonstrates user experience more directly than procurement considerations such as deployment, cost, or IT management.

**Trade-off:** Broad emotional accessibility, but less direct support for technical decision-making.

### Weakness 3 — Commercial Attribution

A memorable brand film does not automatically establish that viewers are more likely to adopt Apple products at work.

**Trade-off:** Strong storytelling potential, but business impact requires additional measurement.

These are analytical limitations and potential risks, not verified campaign failures.

## 11. Three Proposed Improvements

### Improvement A — Role-Specific Follow-Up Films

Create short follow-up executions for distinct workplace audiences, such as designers, operations teams, and small-business owners.

**Why:** Translate the general ecosystem story into more specific use cases.

**Trade-off:** More production work and potentially less universal storytelling.

### Improvement B — Interactive Workflow Exploration

Develop a campaign landing page where users can explore the tools used during different stages of the pizza box project.

**Why:** Connect entertainment to deeper product understanding.

**Trade-off:** Requires a clear user experience and reliable product information.

### Improvement C — Business Decision-Maker Retargeting

Follow the brand film with concise, evidence-backed materials covering deployment, management, compatibility, and support.

**Why:** Address questions that the entertainment-led film does not attempt to answer.

**Trade-off:** Less emotionally engaging creative, but potentially greater relevance deeper in the buying journey.

These are independently proposed extensions, not documented elements of the original campaign.

## 12. Alternative Creative Direction

### Proposed Concept: The Invisible Team Member

**Campaign proposition:** Great tools don't demand attention. They help great work happen.

#### Film Idea

A small creative agency prepares for a high-stakes client presentation.

Throughout the day, the camera follows the team's progress.

Files move between devices, colleagues collaborate remotely, and ideas develop across different work environments.

The audience gradually notices a pattern:

The technology is always present, but never interrupts the story.

The final presentation begins.

A team member is asked how they managed everything.

She looks at her colleagues.

"We worked together."

**End frame:**

Apple at Work

*The best tools let the work speak.*

This is an original speculative concept, not an official Apple advertisement or tagline.

### Strategic Difference

*The Underdogs* uses visible workplace chaos and comedy.

*The Invisible Team Member* would use understated execution and quiet competence.

Both communicate collaboration, but through different emotional mechanisms.

## 13. Experiment Plan

### Research Question

Does a character-driven workplace story improve consideration of Apple's business ecosystem compared with a conventional product-feature advertisement?

### Hypothesis

Narrative-led creative generates greater business-product consideration than a feature-led execution among relevant professional audiences.

### Test Design

**Control:** Feature-led product demonstration.

**Treatment:** Narrative-led workplace film.

**Primary KPI:** Consideration lift for Apple products in workplace use.

**Secondary KPIs:**
- Correct brand attribution.
- Message comprehension.
- Qualified visits to business-product pages.
- Video completion.

### Measurement

Use randomized exposed and control groups where feasible.

Conduct a brand-lift study with appropriate audience qualification.

Determine sample size using baseline consideration, minimum detectable effect, statistical power, and significance level.

No sample size or effectiveness outcome is assumed.

### Interpretation

A higher video completion rate would not by itself demonstrate greater business consideration.

A statistically significant difference in consideration would support the campaign hypothesis, subject to the limitations of the study design.

## 14. Evidence of Recognition Versus Effectiveness

The campaign received industry recognition, including awards documented by D&AD and The One Show.

These awards provide evidence of creative recognition.

They do not independently establish:
- Incremental enterprise sales.
- Increased business market share.
- Improved customer acquisition cost.
- Return on advertising investment.

Such outcomes would require separate commercial evidence.

## 15. Key Lessons for MarketingOps Kit

1. Product demonstration can be embedded in a story rather than separated from it.
2. Humour can make complex B2B propositions more approachable.
3. Brand integration is stronger when the product affects the plot.
4. Creative recognition and commercial effectiveness must be evaluated separately.
5. Campaign recommendations should connect creative changes to measurable business questions.
