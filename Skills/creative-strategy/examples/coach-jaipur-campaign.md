# Coach × Jaipur — Not Born to Follow

**Independent Speculative Campaign | India | 2026**

> A proposed India-specific luxury marketing campaign developed as a creative strategy portfolio exercise. This is not an official Coach project. No royal-family partnership, endorsement, licensing arrangement, or campaign execution is claimed.

## 1. Executive Summary

**Campaign title:** Not Born to Follow

**Proposed brand:** Coach

**Market:** India

**Potential collaborators:** Representatives of Jaipur's former royal family and local cultural partners, subject to agreement and appropriate permissions.

**Campaign proposition:** You inherit a story. You choose what comes next.

**Strategic objective:** Build relevance among younger Indian luxury consumers by connecting Coach with contemporary individuality and cultural self-expression.

**Core execution:** A cinematic fashion film supported by an editorial portrait series, a proposed cultural event, and retail activation.

## 2. Business Challenge

The proposed challenge is to develop an India-specific campaign that communicates Coach's value beyond product visibility and conventional luxury status signaling.

The campaign should:

- Establish an emotionally distinctive message.
- Connect international brand identity with an Indian cultural context.
- Avoid superficial use of Indian heritage.
- Create opportunities for qualified product consideration.
- Remain consistent with Coach's broader positioning.

This is an independently framed strategic brief, not a confirmed internal Coach business problem.

## 3. Target Audience

### Primary Audience

Young adult Indian consumers interested in premium fashion, design, contemporary culture, and personal expression.

### Secondary Audience

Established luxury shoppers who value craftsmanship, cultural storytelling, and distinctive accessories.

### Research Hypotheses

- Some younger luxury consumers may value individuality more than conventional status cues.
- Contemporary interpretations of heritage may appeal to consumers navigating tradition and modern identity.
- Authentic cultural participation may create stronger engagement than decorative use of heritage imagery.

These hypotheses require validation through consumer interviews, social listening, and relevant category research.

## 4. Strategic Tension

**The identity you're given versus the identity you create.**

A person may inherit a family name, cultural history, or social expectations without wanting those elements to define every future choice.

The campaign dramatizes that tension through a young protagonist moving between traditional portraiture and contemporary self-expression.

### Single-Minded Proposition

**You inherit a story. You choose what comes next.**

### Proposed Campaign Title

**NOT BORN TO FOLLOW**

### Brand Role

Coach becomes a personal expression of the protagonist's chosen identity.

The product is part of her contemporary style rather than a substitute for her personality.

## 5. Hero Film: The Portrait

**Format:** 75-second cinematic film

**Setting:** A Jaipur heritage property, subject to location permissions.

**Genre:** Contemporary luxury fashion storytelling.

### Scene 1 — The Inheritance

A young woman walks through a grand corridor lined with ancestral portraits.

The environment is formal and quiet.

**Voiceover:**

"Who are you, when the world already knows your name?"

The camera studies the portraits and their ceremonial details.

### Scene 2 — The Expectation

The protagonist approaches an empty portrait frame.

An attendant presents a formal ceremonial outfit.

She pauses, smiles, and declines.

The moment is respectful, not dismissive of tradition.

### Scene 3 — The Choice

She chooses a contemporary outfit and a Coach bag.

The film's visual language shifts from rigid compositions to more natural movement.

She walks toward an open courtyard.

### Scene 4 — The Living Generation

The courtyard is filled with contemporary artists, musicians, designers, and friends.

The protagonist joins the gathering without ceremony.

The focus is on participation rather than status.

### Scene 5 — The Portrait

The camera returns to the empty frame.

It now contains a candid contemporary portrait of the protagonist and her friends.

**Voiceover:**

"Some stories are inherited. The most important ones are lived."

**End frame:**

COACH

NOT BORN TO FOLLOW

You inherit a story. You choose what comes next.

## 6. Creative Rationale

### The Portrait Frame

Represents inherited identity, public expectations, and established conventions.

### The Contemporary Portrait

Represents self-definition, participation, and an evolving cultural legacy.

### The Coach Accessory

Acts as a visible styling choice within the protagonist's self-expression.

### The Palace Setting

Creates a recognizable visual contrast between historical legacy and contemporary culture.

### The Royal-Family Collaboration

Could make the theme more compelling if participants genuinely connect with the message.

However, the creative idea must not depend entirely on celebrity visibility or inherited status.

## 7. Integrated Activation Plan

### A. Hero Film

**Channels:** YouTube, Instagram, selected premium digital video placements, and suitable brand-owned channels.

**Purpose:** Build campaign awareness and communicate the central proposition.

### B. Portraits of Tomorrow

A proposed editorial series featuring emerging Indian creative professionals.

Each participant responds to:

"What were you expected to become, and what did you choose?"

**Purpose:** Extend the campaign beyond its hero film and create more diverse representations of individuality.

### C. The Unwritten Gallery

A proposed cultural installation in Jaipur.

Potential components:

- Contemporary portrait exhibitions.
- Conversations with emerging creatives.
- Fashion and design presentations.
- A curated Coach product showcase.

**Purpose:** Generate cultural participation, earned attention, and qualified engagement.

### D. Retail Integration

Proposed in-store campaign displays and digital editorial experiences featuring campaign portraits and selected accessories.

**Purpose:** Connect the campaign narrative with product discovery.

## 9. Measurement Framework

### Brand Objectives

- Improve awareness among relevant audiences.
- Strengthen association with individuality.
- Increase consideration among exposed consumers.

### Commercial Objectives

- Increase qualified visits to featured product pages.
- Support accessory consideration and retail discovery.
- Evaluate incremental purchases where feasible.

### Suggested KPIs

**Brand:** Unaided and aided recall, message association, consideration lift.

**Content:** Qualified reach, completion rate, engaged viewing, editorial engagement.

**Retail:** Product-page visits, store visits where measurable, conversion rate, incremental sales.

**Experiential:** Relevant attendance, qualified participation, post-event consideration.

### Measurement Limitations

Campaign exposure does not automatically establish incremental business impact.

Where feasible, use controlled brand-lift studies, comparable markets, or appropriate holdout methods.

Do not attribute all observed sales changes to the campaign.

## 10. Proposed Experiments

### Experiment A: Creative Message

**Hypothesis:** Identity-led storytelling creates stronger brand consideration than conventional product-led luxury advertising.

**Method:** Compare the proposed identity-led execution against an appropriate product-led creative control.

**Primary metric:** Brand consideration lift.

### Experiment B: Editorial Participation

**Hypothesis:** Personal stories from emerging creatives generate stronger qualified engagement than conventional fashion editorial content.

**Method:** Compare editorial formats across comparable audiences and placements.

**Primary metric:** Qualified engagement rate, defined before launch.

### Experiment C: Retail Connection

**Hypothesis:** Campaign storytelling integrated into product discovery improves consideration relative to standard product presentation.

**Method:** Controlled digital or retail test, subject to operational feasibility.

**Primary metric:** Product consideration or conversion, depending on the test design.

## 12. Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Proposed mitigation |
|---|---|
| Royal heritage overwhelms the Coach brand | Keep the protagonist's personal choices central |
| Campaign appears elitist | Include emerging creatives from varied backgrounds |
| Heritage becomes decorative | Ground the story in contemporary lived experiences |
| Product visibility is weak | Give the Coach accessory a clear narrative role |
| Collaboration cannot be secured | Develop an alternative casting and location concept |
| High production costs | Prepare reduced-scope film and digital-only options |
| Weak commercial connection | Integrate product discovery and test measurable outcomes |

## 13. Strategic Recommendation

Proceed to audience validation and partnership feasibility before production.

If supported by research, develop the film and editorial portrait series as the campaign's foundation.

Treat the proposed gallery as an optional extension dependent on cost, partner participation, and measurable value.

The campaign should be evaluated for both brand impact and commercial relevance.
