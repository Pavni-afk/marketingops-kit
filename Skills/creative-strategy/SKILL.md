# Creative Strategy

## Objective

Turn a business problem into a differentiated, actionable advertising strategy.

Do not begin with taglines or slogans. Start with the business objective, audience, and evidence.

## Step 1: Understand the business

Identify:

- Brand and product
- Business objective
- Target audience
- Competitors
- Product differentiation
- Budget and timeline
- Available research

If information is missing, explicitly label assumptions.

## Step 2: Identify the audience insight

Investigate:

- Functional needs
- Emotional motivations
- Purchase barriers
- Existing alternatives
- Relevant cultural context

Separate verified insights from hypotheses.

## Step 3: Find the strategic tension

Identify a contradiction, overlooked need, or audience belief that the brand can credibly address.

Explain why the tension matters to the campaign objective.

## Step 4: Develop three creative territories

For each territory, provide:

1. Territory name
2. Audience insight
3. Central campaign idea
4. Key message
5. Reason to believe
6. Example advertisement
7. Potential weakness

Ensure all three territories are meaningfully different.

## Step 5: Evaluate the territories

Score each territory from 1 to 5 for:

- Audience relevance
- Brand fit
- Differentiation
- Execution feasibility
- Testability

Explain the reasoning behind each score.

These are qualitative judgments, not measured campaign results.

## Step 6: Create the creative brief

Include:

- Business problem
- Communication objective
- Target audience
- Audience insight
- Strategic tension
- Single-minded proposition
- Reasons to believe
- Creative direction
- Deliverables
- Constraints

## Step 7: Design a test

Recommend:

- Campaign hypothesis
- Creative variable to test
- Primary KPI
- Secondary KPIs
- Comparison method
- Decision criteria

Never invent expected conversion improvements.

## Required output

Produce:

1. Problem definition
2. Audience analysis
3. Strategic tension
4. Three creative territories
5. Evaluation matrix
6. Recommended direction
7. Creative brief
8. Experiment plan
9. Risks and unanswered questions

## Quality standards

Before finishing, verify:

- Assumptions are clearly identified.
- Recommendations address the business objective.
- Creative ideas are strategically distinct.
- Claims are supported by available evidence.
- Execution fits the budget and channels.
- Testing recommendations are measurable.

## Research integrity

Never fabricate customer research, market statistics, competitor campaigns, testimonials, or performance metrics.

If evidence is missing, state what research is needed.

## Mandatory Input Fidelity and Validation

### 1. Extract the source brief

Before developing a strategy, create a Source Brief
Ledger containing:

- Brand
- Product or service
- Market and geography
- Target audience
- Business objective
- Budget
- Timeline
- Available channels
- Available evidence

Copy these details from the supplied brief.
Do not replace them with inferred alternatives.

### 2. Preserve the source brief

Treat the user's supplied information as authoritative
for the assignment.

Never silently change:

- Brand or product category
- Audience
- Geography
- Business objective
- Budget
- Timeline
- Channel constraints

If information is missing, mark it "Not provided."

Do not invent a replacement business context.

### 3. Stop on critical input conflicts

If the source documents contain conflicting
brand, product, or audience information:

- Identify the conflict.
- Ask for clarification.
- Do not produce a completed campaign for
  an invented or unrelated business.

### 4. Mandatory strategy evaluation

Evaluate all three creative territories using
exactly these five criteria:

1. Audience relevance
2. Brand fit
3. Differentiation
4. Execution feasibility
5. Testability

Score each from 1 to 5.

Provide a brief justification for each score.

Label scores as subjective strategic judgments.

### 5. Evidence and claims

Classify important statements as:

- Supplied fact
- Verified external fact
- Assumption
- Hypothesis
- Recommendation

Do not invent research findings, conversion rates,
competitor performance, customer testimonials,
or expected campaign improvements.

Numerical targets must be supported by a
source, baseline, or explicitly labeled
planning assumption.

### 6. Experiment validity

An experiment must identify:

- Hypothesis
- Control
- Treatment
- Primary metric
- Secondary metrics
- Measurement window
- Sample-size planning method
- Decision rule

Never treat an arbitrary impression count
as proof of statistical significance.

### 7. Final source-brief audit

Before delivering the strategy, compare the
final output with the Source Brief Ledger.

Verify:

- [ ] Correct brand
- [ ] Correct product
- [ ] Correct audience
- [ ] Correct geography
- [ ] Correct business objective
- [ ] Budget respected
- [ ] Timeline respected
- [ ] Channels respected
- [ ] Three distinct creative territories
- [ ] Five required evaluation criteria
- [ ] No fabricated evidence
- [ ] Experiment is measurable

If a critical field does not match, revise
the output before presenting it.

### 8. Self-assessment integrity

Never claim a requirement passed without
checking it against the original brief.

Report failures explicitly.

A polished response that addresses the wrong
business problem is a failed response.

## Commercial and Evidence Quality Gates

### Product Claims
Never invent product features, specifications,
certifications, or performance claims.

If a feature is not supplied or independently
verified, label it "Requires verification."

### Budget Allocation
When a budget is provided, create an allocation
covering all relevant expenses, including:

- Creative production
- Paid media
- Influencers or partners
- Experiential activations
- Measurement
- Contingency

The total must not exceed the stated budget.

### KPI Alignment
The primary KPI must measure the stated
business objective.

For brand consideration objectives, prioritize
appropriately designed consideration-lift
measurement.

Treat CTR, impressions, and engagement as
supporting indicators, not direct substitutes.

### Experiment Design
Never claim that equal budget distribution
guarantees statistical validity.

Determine sample size using baseline rates,
minimum detectable effect, statistical power,
and significance level.

If these inputs are missing, state that the
sample size cannot yet be determined.

### Final Quality Gate
Before delivering, verify:

- [ ] No unsupported product claims
- [ ] Budget allocations reconcile
- [ ] Primary KPI matches the business objective
- [ ] Experiment sample-size assumptions are valid
- [ ] No fabricated commercial outcomes
